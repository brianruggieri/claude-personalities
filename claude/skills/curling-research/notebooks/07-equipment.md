# Equipment

> Material science, regulations, and manufacturing for everything a curler
> touches: stones, brooms, footwear, stabilizers, measures, ice tools, and
> apparel. Spans on-ice tools (running bands, slider PTFE, pebble cans)
> and off-ice gear (delivery sticks, jerseys). Excludes the *technique* of
> sweeping (Notebook 3), the *technique* of ice prep (Notebook 8), and the
> *shot effects* of equipment (Notebook 4). When a topic is dual-coded
> (e.g. "what fabric is on a brush head" vs "what does sweeping with that
> fabric do to the stone"), the *fabric and certification* lives here, the
> *physical effect* lives in Notebook 3.

## Scope summary

- **Stones** — granite petrology (Ailsa Craig Blue Hone, Ailsa Craig
  Common Green, Trefor Blue, Trefor Red), Kays of Scotland manufacture,
  running-band geometry, handle hardware, weight/diameter regulations,
  stone certification.
- **Brooms** — post-2016 World Curling brush directive, BalancePlus /
  Goldline / Hardline product evolution, approved fabrics, faceplate
  systems, foam densities, durability.
- **Footwear** — slider materials (red brick, PTFE/Teflon, stainless
  steel), gripper styles, hack-shoe construction, BalancePlus/Asham/Goldline
  shoe lines, slider thickness ladder.
- **Stabilizers** — hog stick, balance assist, sliding stabilizers
  (older curlers, wheelchair curlers), delivery sticks (mixed doubles +
  stick curling).
- **Measuring tools** — 6-foot measure, biter stick, laser/digital
  measures, eye-line measures.
- **Ice tools** — pebble cans (coarse/fine, multiple head sizes), the
  Nipper (manual + motorised), Ice King profiler line, scrapers, hack
  hardware.
- **Apparel** — gloves, base layers, regulation jerseys, footwear-specific
  socks.
- **Mixed-doubles delivery sticks** — telescoping pole regulations and
  the wheelchair-stick lineage.

## 1. Stones — granite, geometry, manufacture

### Ailsa Craig and the only manufacturer

1. **[Kays Scotland — Curling Stones product page](https://www.kaysscotland.com/curling-stones)** — the canonical
   source. Two granites: Common Green for the body (heat-resistant,
   anti-splintering on collisions) and Blue Hone for the running edge
   ("Ailserts," single or double insert per face). Stones with non-Blue-Hone
   running edges *pit*, which compromises performance. Kays has supplied
   the last five Olympic Winter Games.
2. **[Kays Scotland — Why Kays](https://www.kaysscotland.com/why-kays)** — exclusive harvesting rights to
   Ailsa Craig granite (granted by the Marquess of Ailsa). Sole supplier
   of WCF-certified competition stones.
3. **[World Curling — Made in Scotland, built for champions
   (Milano-Cortina 2026 stone feature)](https://worldcurling.org/2026/02/olympic-curling-stone-feature/)** — primary-source
   provenance documentation for the Olympic stone set, including the
   Ailserts process and modern QC.
4. **[Team GB — Ailsa Craig: Everything you need to know](https://www.teamgb.com/article/ailsa-craig-everything-you-need-to-know-about-where-olympic-curling-stones/5EFRzBr6xItMWCr2XRyaTS)**
   — Ailsa Craig is an RSPB wildlife reserve; blasting is restricted.
   2002 and 2013 collections (1,500 t and 2,000 t respectively) supply
   modern stones; the 2013 haul is expected to yield ~10,000 stones.
5. **[Scotsman — How a small Scottish business has been creating curling
   stones for the Olympics for over a hundred years](https://www.scotsman.com/news/scotland/curling-stones-olympics-kays-scotland-5504684)** —
   workshop economics: six craftsmen, ~5 stones/day, ~6 hours/stone,
   pumice + diamond-pad polish.
6. **[MTDCNC — Iconic Scottish Stones Take Center Stage at Winter
   Olympics](https://mtdcnc.com/news/mtdcnc/iconic-scottish-stones-take-center-stage-at-winter-olympics/)** — CNC-assisted shaping detail and quality-check
   protocol for Olympic-grade stones.
7. **[British Curling Supplies — From Island to Ice: a Journey of
   Curling Stones](https://britishcurlingsupplies.co.uk/from-island-to-ice-a-journey-of-curling-stones/)** — supply-chain narrative from quarry boulder to
   finished stone, including transport and inspection steps.

### Trefor (Wales) granite

8. **[Wikipedia — Curling (single allowed Wikipedia link)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling)** — concise summary
   of the Trefor (Trefor Blue, Trefor Red) Welsh granite source as the
   secondary supplier of competition-grade running-band material. Kept
   here because Trefor's first-party online presence is thin; this is
   the single-Wikipedia-link entry for the notebook.

### Stone regulations and dimensions

9. **[World Curling — Rules of Curling 2024 (PDF)](https://worldcurling.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Rules-2024.pdf)** —
   primary source. R2 covers stones: weight 17.24–19.96 kg
   (38–44 lb) including handle and bolt; max circumference 914 mm
   (36 in); min height 114 mm (4.5 in).
10. **[ISF — Curling Technical Rules and Regulations (Jan 2023, PDF)](https://www.isfsports.org/sites/default/files/documents/2023-01/Curling%20%20-%20Technical%20Rules%20and%20Regulations.pdf)**
    — independent restatement of stone specs, useful when WCF rule
    numbering changes between editions.
11. **[Scottish Curling — Rule Book 2019–2020 (PDF)](https://www.scottishcurling.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Rule-Book-2019-2020-FINAL.pdf)** — member-association
    handbook with the same dimensional envelope plus handle/electronics
    (hog-line sensor) provisions.

### Running band geometry and wear

12. **[Nyberg, Hinrichsen, Lundberg — *The importance of the surface
    roughness and running band area on the bottom of a stone for the
    curling phenomenon* (Nature Scientific Reports, 2020)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-76660-8)** — the
    canonical materials-science paper. Running band ≈6 mm wide, ≈120 mm
    diameter, ring-shaped contact area; surface roughness drives the
    asymmetric scratch-guiding effect. Cite this for any "what does the
    bottom of a stone actually look like" question.
13. **[Shegelski & Lozowski — *The asymmetrical friction mechanism that
    puts the curl in the curling stone* (Wear, 2013, PDF mirror)](https://www.thesalmons.org/lynn/curling/A5.pdf)** —
    canonical pressure-difference / scratch-guiding model paper. Any
    discussion of *why* a particular running-band geometry curls a
    specific way starts here.
14. **[Penner — *Dynamics and curl ratio of a curling stone* (Sports
    Engineering, Springer, 2013)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12283-013-0129-8)** — analytic geometry for
    running-band radius vs curl ratio; informs stone-design choices like
    the standard 6 cm running-band radius.
15. **[Throwing Rocks — *We are getting closer to understanding the
    physics of curling* (Glenn Paulley, March 2025)](https://glennpaulley.ca/curling/2025/03/27/we-are-getting-closer-to-understanding-the-physics-of-curling/)** — current
    consensus survey synthesising Nyberg + Shegelski + Penner; useful
    for "which model is currently winning" questions before re-reading
    the primary papers.

    a manufacturer's first-person retrospective on the directional-fabric

20. **[The Grand Slam of Curling — World Curling adds infractions for

## 3. Footwear — sliders, grippers, hack shoes

### Slider materials and the deceleration ladder

26. **[BalancePlus — Slider Deceleration Testing](https://balanceplus.com/deceleration-testing/)** — the
    manufacturer's Doppler-radar bench (130 readings/sec, km/h/s units)
    comparing 1/16" Stealth, 5/32" + toe-coat, ¼" + toe-coat-with-holes,
    and stainless-steel sliders. Confirms PTFE's low-friction
    monotonicity vs steel's scratch-driven deceleration drift.
27. **[Throwing Rocks — Curling shoes: choosing a slider (Glenn
    Paulley)](https://glennpaulley.ca/curling/2011/05/22/curling-shoes-choosing-a-slider/)** —
    the canonical write-up of the *speed ladder*: red brick (slowest,
    striped hard plastic) → 5/32" PTFE (medium) → thicker PTFE → stainless
    steel (fastest). PTFE's self-smoothing scratch behaviour is why it
    dominates competition.
28. **[BalancePlus — Pull-On Slider](https://balanceplus.com/product/pull-on-slider/)** — full-sole 1/16" (1.5 mm)
    Stealth PTFE; entry-level slip-over solution for grippered shoes.
29. **[BalancePlus — Step-On Slider](https://balanceplus.com/product/step-on-slider/)** — full-sole 1/16" Stealth
    PTFE, alternative attachment style; useful when documenting the two
    consumer slider archetypes.

### Hack shoes (uppers + welt construction)

30. **[BalancePlus — Delux Curling Shoes](https://balanceplus.com/product/delux-curling-shoes/)** — full-grain
    leather upper, welted construction, leather-lined interior. The
    Delux line ships in four slider thicknesses: 1/16" (slowest/control),
    1/8", 3/16", 1/4" (fastest). This is the canonical thickness ladder
    a coach references when fitting a new player.
31. **[BalancePlus — Curling Shoes (category)](https://balanceplus.com/product-category/curling-shoes/)** —
    full footwear category including the 100/200/300/400 series; useful
    when comparing women's-fit (400) vs men's-fit (Delux) construction.
32. **[BalancePlus — FAQ](https://balanceplus.com/frequently-asked-questions/)** — manufacturer FAQ on slider
    care, gripper replacement, and hack-shoe sizing — the de-facto
    consumer-care reference.

## 5. Ice tools — pebble cans, profilers, scrapers

38. **[Canada Curling Stone — The 60-Inch Curling Ice Pebble Nipper](https://canadacurlingstone.on.ca/ice-equipment/the-60-inch-nipper/)** —
    primary source on the Nipper: 60" three-blade and 80" four-blade
    variants, used at Brier, Worlds, Salt Lake 2002, Torino 2006. The
    Nipper *cuts* high pebble crowns rather than smashing them
    ("rocking"), giving a flatter run for stones.
39. **[Canada Curling Stone — Curling Ice Equipment (category)](https://canadacurlingstone.on.ca/curling-ice-equipment/)** —
    full ice-tech catalogue: pebble cans, pebble heads (coarse/fine),
    nippers, scrapers, hack hardware. Useful as a ground-truth list
    when sanity-checking what tools an ice tech actually owns.
40. **[Canada Curling Stone — Curling Ice Scrapers & Blades](https://canadacurlingstone.on.ca/curling-ice-scrapers-blades/)** —
    blade and scraper specs (width, blade-edge geometry) for the
    morning ice-prep cut.
41. **[Ice King](https://www.iceking.ca/)** — Canadian profiler manufacturer used by major
    facilities. Equipment includes the Ice King profiler family
    (digitally-controlled drive, oilless gearbox, direct-drive motor)
    and the recent **REGENT** model. The website's deep specs are
    truncated by anti-bot pages; use the Guspro deep-dive below for a
    catalogue summary.
42. **[Guspro — Mastering the Pebble With ICE KING Curling Profilers](https://www.guspro.com/post/mastering-the-pebble-with-ice-king-curling-profilers)** —
    distributor write-up confirming Ice King's profiler architecture
    (digital drive, ultra-quiet motor) and naming the REGENT line.
43. **[Curling Baron — Pebble / Nip making-ice page](https://curlingbaron.wordpress.com/making-ice/)** — practitioner-side
    walkthrough of pebble cans, head sizes, and water-volume markings
    on the can; the de-facto entry-level reference for "how a club ice
    tech actually uses this gear."
44. **[GNCC / SCIG — Cutting Equipment Overview (PDF)](https://gncc.org/~gnccorg/images/pdfs/Training/SCIG_Cutting_Equipment_June_2009.pdf)** —
    Scottish Curling Ice Group's training PDF on scrapers and shaver
    blades (manufacturers, blade geometry, sharpening cadence). Pair
    with the Cutting Technique PDF below for rationale.
45. **[GNCC / SCIG — Cutting Technique (PDF, mirrored on iceking.ca)](https://iceking.ca/icetechblog/CuttingTechnique.pdf)** —
    companion technique doc; the *equipment* angle is the catalogue,
    the *technique* lives in Notebook 8.
46. **[GNCC — Pebble: The Basics (PDF)](https://www.gncc.org/~gnccorg/images/pdfs/Training/Pebble_The_Basics_03152021.pdf)** — pebble-can volume-marking
    procedure, head sizes (coarse for first pass, fine for top), and
    the timing convention for spraying a sheet.

## Notes on coverage

- **Mixed-doubles delivery sticks** are documented under §4 (sources
  33–37) — the equipment is identical in form to wheelchair sticks but
  governed by Notebook 1's rule references for in-game legality.
- **Apparel** (gloves, base layers, jerseys, footwear-specific socks) is
  covered indirectly through manufacturer category pages and the WCF
  rulebook — see Asham's catalogue and the WCF Rules 2024 PDF (R6 player

  warranted at this time; flagged in the Gaps section if the

  covered in §1 of the WCF Rules 2024 PDF (R12 measuring) — the
  *equipment* is rule-driven; specialist measurement-tool catalogues
  add little for our use case.

- Direct first-party Trefor (Wales) granite manufacturer documentation
  is thin; we currently rely on Wikipedia and rule books to cite
  Trefor as a secondary running-band material. If a stone-physics
  question turns on Trefor Blue vs Trefor Red specifically, escalate
  to the Curling Physics Research NotebookLM for primary geological
  references.
- The Royal Caledonian Curling Club's historical equipment archive
  (apparel, antique stones, regional handle hardware) is unscraped
  here. If Notebook 10 (Culture/History) needs deep apparel evolution

- Ice King's full profiler product matrix (model SKUs, blade widths,
