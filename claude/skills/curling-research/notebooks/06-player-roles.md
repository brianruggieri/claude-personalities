# Player Roles & Team Dynamics

**Scope.** How a curling team is structured and how each position contributes
differently. Covers the four traditional positions (lead, second, third / vice-skip,
skip), the alternate, the bench coach, mixed doubles, and wheelchair curling.
Also covers communication patterns, team-cohesion research, and player archetypes.

**Out of scope.** Delivery biomechanics (Notebook 02 / 07), sweeping mechanics
(Notebook 03), shot taxonomy (Notebook 04), and *what* strategic decision a
skip makes (Notebook 05 — this notebook covers *who* makes which decision).

## 1. Canonical Position Map (4-player team)

Stone-delivery order is fixed within an end: each player throws two consecutive
stones, with the skip throwing last. The standard mapping:

| Stones thrown | Position           | Sweeps                     | Calls line | Holds broom |
|---------------|--------------------|----------------------------|-----------|-------------|
| 1, 2          | Lead               | Stones 3–8 (six stones)    | No        | No          |
| 3, 4          | Second             | Stones 1, 2, 5–8           | No        | No          |
| 5, 6          | Third / Vice-Skip  | Stones 1–4 + skip's 7, 8   | When skip throws | When skip throws |
| 7, 8          | Skip               | Almost never sweeps        | Default caller | Default broom-holder |

Skip throws last as the default — but is permitted to play any position
(see §3 below). Any rotation change must be documented before the game starts;
inside a game it can only happen when an alternate enters at the start of an end.

## 2. Position Briefs

### 2.1 Lead

- **Shot load:** stones 1 and 2 — typically guards (centre / corner) and
  draws into the rings. Rarely asked to throw heavy takeouts at the elite level.
- **Sweep load:** highest on the team — sweeps every stone after their own pair
  (stones 3 through 8, six stones per end). Lead fitness and brushing endurance
  are load-bearing for team performance.
- **Modern wrinkle — single-sweeper era:** Team Schwaller (Switzerland) made
  lead Pablo Lachat the *primary* sweeper for almost every shot, including
  the second's, third's and skip's stones. The teammate's stated rationale:
  "Pablo is way more effective than me in most situations… there is no reason
  to miss out on his talent." World Curling reports the practice is widening
  among elite teams; the off-side sweeper has measurably less effect on the
  stone, so concentrating sweep authority on the strongest brusher is now
  considered rational.
- **Archetype:** "draw specialist" — guards must be precise to avoid
  free-guard-zone removals, and exceptional sweeping endurance is non-negotiable.
- **Entry-level position:** clubs typically place new curlers at lead first;
  it has the simplest tactical decision-tree but the heaviest physical load.

### 2.2 Second

- **Shot load:** stones 3 and 4 — first aggressive moves of the end. Often
  the first hit or come-around. Must be strong on takeouts and capable of
  heavy weight while still hitting the broom.
- **Sweep load:** sweeps lead's two stones, then the back-end's four (stones
  5–8). Roughly five sweeps per end.
- **Archetype:** "takeout specialist" — the second is where the team's
  hardest-throwing weight curler typically slots, balancing the lead's finesse
  with raw force and runback ability.
- Russ Howard's *Curl to Win* (chapter 6) features Mark Dacey, 2004 Brier
  champion, on second-position philosophy: maintain the tactical initiatives
  the skip set up and the lead established, and don't try to be the hero —
  the second's missed takeout creates the pressure the third and skip then
  inherit.

### 2.3 Third / Vice-Skip

- **Shot load:** stones 5 and 6 — the most diverse shot palette on the team.
  Freezes, taps, split takeouts, hit-and-rolls, draws to portions of the
  house already congested with stones. Must be a master of the draw and
  capable of tap-up shots that demand fine weight.
- **Sweep load:** sweeps stones 1–4 (the front-end's work) and skip's two
  stones (7 and 8) — but only when the skip is throwing; otherwise the third
  is in the house.
- **Tactical role:** when the skip throws, the third holds the broom, calls
  line, directs the sweepers, and judges weight. Between ends, the third is
  the skip's primary tactical sounding board — the back-end conversation
  during the opponent's stones is "third + skip" almost exclusively.
- **Officiating role:** the *vices* of each team are jointly responsible for
  determining the score at the end of each end, and any in-house measurement
  is conducted by the vices (not the skips). This is a rules-level
  responsibility that survives even at the Olympic / World Championship level.
- **Archetype:** "the glue" — the third is widely described as the most
  well-rounded player on the team. Russ Howard's *Curl to Win* assigns the
  third's chapter to Wade Blanchard (2005 New Brunswick champion).

### 2.4 Skip

- **Shot load:** stones 7 and 8 — the closing shots. Skip's biggest single
  responsibility is "closing the end" with the hammer or limiting damage
  without it. Shot menu is the widest of any position: come-arounds, freezes,
  hit-and-rolls, double takeouts, raise-takeouts, runbacks under pressure.
- **Sweep load:** essentially zero — the skip is the team's quarterback,
  almost always in the house.
- **Strategic authority:** determines weight, turn, line; reads ice; holds
  the broom; calls sweep / no-sweep verbally as the stone travels; selects
  shots from the menu of what's possible given the rocks in play. The team
  must be in sync with the skip's calls and trust the skip's judgement.
- **Russ Howard / Glenn Howard / Niklas Edin lineage:** elite skips are
  characterised by a calm, theory-heavy approach. Edin describes his own
  career as "trying to improve my mental and theoretical game all the time…
  the older I get the more 'mental' I need to look at it." Russ Howard
  pioneered the in-house broom-holding-and-talking style now standard.
- **Eve Muirhead** — 2022 Olympic gold (GBR), 2014 Olympic bronze, multiple
  world / European titles — has been a skip "for as long as she can remember"
  and explicitly treats the role as a leadership-development discipline that
  transferred directly into her post-retirement role as Team GB chef de mission
  at Milano-Cortina 2026.

### 2.5 Alternate (Fifth Player)

- Eligible to substitute for any player. May enter only at the start of an
  end (not mid-end).
- When the alternate enters, the team may rewrite its delivery rotation and
  may also reassign skip / vice-skip duties — applied for the remainder of
  the game. This makes the alternate a strategic lever, not just an injury
  reserve: a skip in poor form can step out and re-enter as third, with the
  vice taking over skip duties for the day.
- *Curl to Win* assigns the alternate chapter to Toby McDonald (1976 Brier,
  later national-team coach), framing the role as "always ready, rarely
  visible" — practice partner, scout, statistician, emotional ballast.

### 2.6 Coach (Bench Coach)

The coach does not play. World / Curling Canada rules give the coach two
structured contact windows during a game:

1. **One 60-second timeout per game** (10-end format; mid-game break also
   exists in some formats). Coach answers tactical questions, reframes the
   ideal-vs-acceptable outcome of the current end, and "briefly discusses
   the options for the next end."
2. **Five-minute halftime / between-end break** in arena settings (with
   chief umpire approval): light feedback, one or two suggestions; not a
   re-strategy session.

Off-ice the coach owns:

- Pre-game prep — meetings to set goals, scouting the opponent, ice-condition
  briefings, lineup decisions.
- Data capture — modern bench coaches use iPads / laptops to capture shot
  call, weight, outcome, miss-direction; Curling Canada NCCP Competition
  Development training drills this practice.
- Season administration — financials, logistics, contracts, parental
  involvement (junior teams), and media management. Curling Canada's
  Competition Development pathway explicitly trains for this scope.
- Between-game scouting — film study and tactical patterning of upcoming
  opponents, especially at Briers / Scotties / Trials / Olympic round-robins.

The bench coach's stated philosophy is to "help the team control the
controllables" — emphasising what's within the athletes' agency rather than
ice or opponent variance.

## 3. Skip-Throws-Third Variants and Position Flexibility

The default is skip-throws-fourth, but rule 1.9-equivalent ("the skip may
play in any position") permits other arrangements. Notable examples
documented in coverage / Wikipedia:

| Skip                 | Threw      | Notes |
|----------------------|------------|-------|
| Randy Ferbey         | Third (5,6)| David Nedohin threw the team's last two stones during the Ferbey Four era — Ferbey held the broom and called the game while Nedohin was the closer. Multiple Brier / World titles. |
| Margaretha Sigfridsson| Lead       | Skipped tactically while throwing first. |
| Peter de Cruz        | Second     | Documented variant. |
| Russ Howard (with Gushue, Torino 2006) | Second | Howard joined Brad Gushue's team as second/coach; Gushue was nominal skip but Howard called the game from second position — Olympic gold. |

**Why decouple shot-calling from last-rock duty?** Calling line and judging
weight from the house requires a specific perceptual skill set; throwing
the closing rock under pressure requires a delivery skill set. They don't
have to live in the same body. The Ferbey-Nedohin model is the canonical
proof.

## 4. Communication Patterns

### 4.1 Skip → sweepers (during stone delivery)

The skip's verbal vocabulary is small and high-information:

- **"Sweep" / "Hurry" / "Hard" / "Hurry hard" / "Yes"** — start sweeping or
  sweep harder.
- **"No" / "Stop" / "Never" / "Off"** (or raised hand) — stop sweeping.
- **"Right off" / "Clean"** — light stone-touching brush only (keeping the
  path clean of debris) without applying weight-changing pressure.

### 4.2 Sweepers → skip (weight calls)

Sweepers, who are physically alongside the moving stone, give the skip a
*weight estimate* using a numeric scale common across the world:

| Number | Stopping zone                 |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| 1–3    | Free guard zone (in front of house) |
| 4–6    | Front rings, ahead of tee     |
| 7      | Tee / on the button           |
| 8–10   | Back rings, behind the tee    |
| 11     | Through the house, out of play |

This lets the skip choose whether to sweep, stay off, or signal "we need
more / less." Numbers travel down the sheet faster than sentences.

### 4.3 Skip → vice (between-stone tactical talk)

Mid-end pauses, especially before the skip's stones, are the team's primary
strategy window. The third is the skip's only sustained tactical interlocutor;
front-end players rarely participate at this level. Between-end discussions
expand to the full team and, on coach-eligible breaks, the coach.

### 4.4 Coach → team (timeouts and breaks)

Strictly bounded: one 60-second timeout, five-minute halftime, between-end
intervals where neither team's clock is running (arena play with umpire
approval).

## 5. Team Chemistry & Sport-Psychology Research

### 5.1 Empathy → cohesion → communication → performance

Bedir, Agduman, Bedir & Erhan (2023, *Frontiers in Psychology*; n = 241
curlers, 69 teams, Turkish Curling League) found:

- Team cohesion **and** empathy both predicted competition performance.
- **Communication skills fully mediated** empathy's effect on performance,
  and partially mediated cohesion's effect.
- Together, the three explained **53% of variance** in competition
  performance — an unusually high effect size for a sport-psychology study.

The practical implication: cohesion alone is not sufficient. Teams must
convert cohesion into *shared, real-time communication* during the
delivery / sweep / call cycle for it to translate to outcomes.

### 5.2 Self-regulation interventions in elite curling teams

Collins & Durand-Bush (2010, *International Journal of Sports Science &
Coaching*) ran a self-regulation intervention with an elite Canadian curling
team. Both the coach and the athletes reported increased cohesion and
performance post-intervention — the study is the most-cited case study on
psychological skill-building in elite curling.

### 5.3 Scoping review (Tutty et al., 2024, *Frontiers in Sports and Active
Living*)

A 94-study scoping review on curling research found:

- Heavy attention on physics / sweeping / delivery biomechanics.
- **Minimal research on positions, leadership, communication, or
  archetypes.** Only one study examined group cohesion (in wheelchair
  curling).
- Mixed doubles received zero peer-reviewed quantitative attention despite
  being an Olympic discipline since 2018.

This is a documented research gap — not an absence of phenomenon. Coaching
manuals and memoirs cover the territory; quantitative sport-science does
not yet.

## 6. Athlete Archetypes

Cross-referencing memoirs, coaching guides and elite-team analyses yields a
small set of recurring archetypes:

| Archetype                       | Typical position(s) | Signature trait |
|---------------------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Draw specialist (finesse)       | Lead, third         | Inch-perfect weight, low variance, low-pressure shot menu |
| Takeout specialist (force)      | Second              | Heavy weight + on-broom accuracy under load |
| All-rounder / "the glue"        | Third               | Full shot menu — the team's safety valve when the skip needs flexibility |
| Tactical skip (theoretical)     | Skip                | Deep ice-reading, calm in-house decision-making — Edin / Russ Howard / Eve Muirhead lineage |
| Technical skip (closer)         | Skip or fourth-thrower | Big-shot delivery under pressure, often paired with a strong third — Nedohin (Ferbey) / Gushue / Jennifer Jones |
| Sweep-engine                    | Lead (modern era)   | Single-sweeper era: the team's brushing leverage, e.g. Pablo Lachat / Team Schwaller |

These are *tendencies*, not assignments. Many elite teams (Edin, Gushue,
Homan, Einarson) will rotate or restructure between seasons or even
between ends-with-alternate scenarios. Position selection at the elite level
is treated as portfolio optimisation: maximise summed shot-percentage given
the team's combined skill vector and the opponent's expected pressure
profile.

## 7. Mixed Doubles (2-Player Teams)

Mixed doubles is structurally different — the rotation rules dissolve the
traditional position taxonomy.

- **2 players per team. 5 stones each per end** (vs 4×2 in standard).
- One player throws **stones 1 and 5** (first and last of the end).
- The other player throws **stones 2, 3, 4** (the middle three).
- The team can **swap which role each player plays at the start of any end** —
  this is a tactical lever not available in 4-player curling.
- **No fixed "skip" position.** The non-throwing player is in the house
  calling the line; the thrower then either sweeps their own rock (with the
  in-house partner skipping) or watches it (with the in-house partner
  sweeping). Authority for calling line passes back and forth.
- **High-level convention** — in elite play, the female partner often throws
  1+5 and the male throws 2–4, but this is purely tactical (not regulated).
- A "powerplay" once per game lets the hammer team pre-place stones in a
  more aggressive position; the role of the placement decision sits with
  whichever player owns that end's strategic call.

## 8. Wheelchair Curling

Wheelchair curling rewrites position semantics because **sweeping is
prohibited** — physically infeasible from a chair, and the sport is
designed around that constraint.

- **Team of four**, with **at least one female athlete on the ice at all
  times** (mixed-team format mandated by World Curling).
- Positions are nominally **lead / second / third / skip** with an
  **alternate**. Skip usually plays fourth but is not required to.
- **No sweepers, no sweep calls.** Once a stone leaves the delivery stick,
  its path is fixed.
- **Delivery uses an extender stick** — the stone is pushed from the
  hack-area while seated. Some athletes also use a slider / cue.
- All four players' value sits in **delivery precision and tactical
  discussion**. The vice still measures, the skip still calls strategy and
  holds the target — but the "weight call" sweeper feedback channel is
  absent. This makes pre-shot calibration and weight-judgement of the
  thrower disproportionately important versus stand-up curling.

## 9. Implications for Sim / Game Design

(Brief notes — full design treatment lives in Notebook 11.)

- **Per-position skill vectors.** Each position has a distinct shot
  distribution and skill emphasis — lead/draw, second/takeout, third/full
  menu, skip/full menu + ice-reading. A roster model with one global
  skill rating misses 70%+ of the structure.
- **Sweep authority is configurable.** Modern elite teams can have a
  primary sweeper (single-sweeper era). The sim should not hardcode
  "lead+second sweep" — Team Schwaller's pattern is now mainstream.
- **Skip ≠ thrower.** Allow the player to designate one player as
  shot-caller and a different player as fourth-stone thrower (Ferbey-Nedohin
  pattern). This is a one-line config in real curling and should be one in
  the sim.
- **Vice owns measurement.** If an in-house measurement subsystem exists
  (touching biters, deciding count), the *third*, not the skip, is the
  authority. Gameplay UX should reflect this.
- **Mixed doubles is not "small 4-player curling."** Stones-1+5 vs middle-3
  rotation, powerplay, and free-flow skip authority make it a separate
  rules surface.
- **Wheelchair mode disables the entire sweeping subsystem** and shifts
  weight-judgement load fully to the delivery model — distinct difficulty
  curve from stand-up.

## Gaps

- **Quantitative position-load data** (stones per position by shot type;
  sweep-distance per position per game) is largely unpublished. World
  Curling broadcasts capture shot-percentage by player but the underlying
  distributions aren't open.
- **Shot-percentage by position over a season for elite teams** would
  improve archetype calibration but is gated by federation-internal data.
- **Mixed doubles role research** is essentially non-existent (Tutty 2024
  scoping review notes zero peer-reviewed studies despite 2018 Olympic
  inclusion).
- **Coach-impact quantification** — does a strong bench coach measurably
  change shot percentage? No published study found.
- **Cross-cultural team-dynamics work** — most cohesion research is on
  Canadian / Turkish samples; no Scottish / Swedish / Japanese / Korean
  comparison study found.
- **Direct interview archives.** A first-pass search did not surface
  long-form Stoughton, Glenn Howard, or Marc Kennedy interviews
  specifically on positional philosophy. The NotebookLM curling research
  corpus may have these — flagged as a follow-up source-add candidate.
